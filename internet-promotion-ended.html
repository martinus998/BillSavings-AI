Your internet promotion ended. Now compare the full price.

Introductory pricing can make an internet plan look inexpensive for months and then disappear on a later statement. Before switching providers, identify the exact discount that ended and compare the new recurring total with any available alternatives.

What usually changes when a promotion expires

Base monthly rate

The most common change is a temporary credit disappearing and the account moving to standard pricing.

Bundle discount

A discount tied to mobile, TV, autopay or another service may also change when the original offer ends.

Equipment discount

Modem, router or Wi-Fi equipment may have been discounted during the promotional period.

New taxes or fees

Some line items can change independently of the promotion, so compare the whole statement rather than only the base price.

Compare the old bill with the new bill

Write down the prior base rate, promotional credit, equipment charges, add-ons, taxes and total. Then compare the same lines on the new statement. If the increase equals the missing promotional credit, you have probably found the main cause. If the increase is larger, another charge may have changed at the same time.

What to ask before accepting another offer

New recurring total

Ask for the full monthly amount after all discounts and required equipment.

Promotion length

Confirm exactly how many months the new discount lasts and what the price becomes afterward.

Plan changes

Check whether the offer changes speed, data limits, equipment or contract terms.

A replacement promotion can help, but the best comparison is the long-term total rather than the first advertised month.

Frequently asked questions

Can I ask for another internet promotion?

You can ask whether current account offers or plan alternatives are available, but eligibility varies by provider and market.

Should I downgrade my speed to save money?

Only if a lower tier still fits your household usage. Compare the full plan terms before changing.

Does BillSavings AI negotiate with providers?

No. It helps organize bill changes and questions so you can review the account yourself.
