Contract fee review

Early termination fee? Check the contract timeline first

Cancellation charges can be confusing because service terms, promotions, equipment and account changes may all overlap. Start by matching the fee to the contract dates, cancellation date and equipment obligations.

Review My Bill

Four details to verify

Contract end date

Find the stated minimum term and confirm whether cancellation happened before it ended.

Cancellation date

Compare the date you requested cancellation with the provider's recorded effective date.

Promotion terms

Some discounts or credits are tied to keeping service for a specific period.

Equipment obligations

Routers, modems, phones or other equipment may have separate return or payoff requirements.

Do not mix separate charges together

An early termination fee may appear beside a device payoff, final-month charge, non-return fee, installation balance or tax. Ask the provider to explain each line separately so you know which amount comes from the contract and which comes from equipment or service.

If rented equipment is involved, compare our equipment rental fee guide. For internet hardware specifically, see the internet equipment fee guide.

Promotion terms can make the timeline harder to read

A discounted monthly rate does not always mean there is no minimum term. The promotion period, contract term and equipment obligation can have different dates.

Compare the promotional end date with the actual contract end date. If your internet price changed recently, the internet promotion ended guide can help separate a price increase from a cancellation charge.

Moving or transferring service may be treated differently

Some providers handle relocation, transfer of service and full cancellation differently. If you are moving, ask whether service can be transferred, whether the term continues and whether unavailable service at the new address changes the cancellation rules.

Do not assume a move automatically removes or triggers a fee. The agreement and provider policy control the account outcome.

Compare the cost of leaving with the savings

If you are switching providers to save money, compare the termination fee, equipment charges, installation costs and any new-provider setup fees with the expected monthly savings.

A lower advertised rate can take months to recover a large switching cost. Our lower monthly bills guide explains why the full transition cost matters.

A simple contract review

1. Find the contract dates

Record the start date, minimum term and stated end date.

2. Mark the cancellation date

Use the provider's recorded effective date, not only the day you called.

3. Separate equipment

Identify any device balance or non-return charge.

4. Check promotions

Confirm whether a discount period is different from the contract term.

5. Look for exceptions

Review relocation, service-availability or other clauses that may apply.

6. Verify the final bill

Confirm that all cancellation, equipment and final-service charges match the explanation.

Questions to ask the provider

  • Which contract clause created this fee?
  • What contract end date do you have on the account?
  • What cancellation date did you record?
  • Is any part of this charge an equipment balance?
  • Were there exceptions or cancellation windows in my agreement?
  • Does moving or transferring service change the fee?
  • Will another final bill or equipment charge arrive later?
  • Is any promotional credit being reversed separately?

Frequently asked questions

Can an early termination fee be waived?

Policies vary by provider and contract. Ask whether the agreement includes any exceptions, but do not assume a waiver is guaranteed.

Is an equipment balance the same as a termination fee?

No. Device payoffs, rental-return charges and contract termination fees can be separate.

Should I cancel before comparing the fee?

It is useful to understand the contract and switching costs before making a final change.

Can BillSavings AI cancel service for me?

No. It helps organize bill and contract-related charges so you can ask better questions.
