Sewer bill diagnosis

Why is my sewer bill so high?

A higher wastewater charge can come from water usage, the utility's averaging method, a rate change, a longer billing cycle, a fixed charge or an account adjustment. Separate those pieces before assuming the bill is wrong.

Review My Utility Bill

Start by finding the billing formula

Sewer billing does not work the same way everywhere. Some utilities use current metered water consumption, others use a winter average, and some combine a fixed base charge with a usage-based component.

Look for language such as wastewater units, sewer usage, winter average, base charge or service charge. If the formula is not clear on the statement, ask the utility which usage value was used.

Compare water use first

If sewer charges are tied to metered water, compare current water consumption with the previous bill and with the same season last year. A higher water reading can raise both the water and sewer portions of the account.

Check whether the utility uses an average

Some wastewater systems use water consumption from selected winter months or another historical period to estimate indoor sewer use. That means the current sewer amount may not move in step with this month's water consumption.

Find the average period, the usage value being applied and when the utility recalculates it. A new annual average can create a noticeable change even if this month's habits are unchanged.

Five major reasons sewer bills change

Higher water consumption

When sewer is tied to metered water, more household consumption can increase wastewater charges too.

New average

A newly calculated winter or historical average can change the sewer usage value for future bills.

Rate increase

The wastewater price per unit can change even when the billed usage stays similar.

Fixed-charge change

Base, infrastructure or service charges may rise independently of usage.

Longer billing period

More billing days can increase usage-based charges even when daily consumption remains similar.

Adjustment or correction

A corrected meter reading, prior estimate or account adjustment can move charges into the current statement.

Look for leaks when water use also increased

If both water and sewer usage rose unexpectedly, check for toilets that run, dripping fixtures, irrigation issues or other continuous water use. A small leak that runs all day can affect the metered total more than an occasional visible drip.

If a verified leak caused unusual consumption, ask whether the utility has a leak-adjustment policy and whether sewer charges are included in that process. Eligibility and documentation requirements vary.

Normalize for billing days

Compare usage per day when the statement lengths differ. A longer billing period can make the total look unusually high without a comparable increase in daily consumption.

Separate rate changes from usage changes

Write down sewer units and the rate per unit from the current and prior statements. If units are similar but the total increased, the rate or fixed charge may be the cause. If the rate is unchanged but units rose, focus on the usage calculation.

This makes a utility call more specific because you can ask about the exact line that changed rather than only the final total.

Check for estimated or corrected meter readings

If water readings feed the sewer calculation, an estimated reading followed by an actual correction can affect wastewater charges too. Review the meter-reading label and service dates.

A seven-step sewer bill review

1. Find the sewer formula

Determine whether billing uses current water use, an average or both.

2. Compare usage

Check water and sewer units against prior periods.

3. Check billing days

Compare average daily use if cycles differ.

4. Compare rates

Look for price-per-unit changes.

5. Review fixed charges

Check base and infrastructure fees separately.

6. Look for adjustments

Identify corrections, estimates or annual recalculations.

7. Verify the next bill

Confirm any correction or adjustment appears as expected.

Questions to ask the utility

  • What exact usage value was used to calculate my sewer charge?
  • Is the bill based on current water use or an average?
  • What months are used for that average?
  • Did the sewer rate or fixed service charge change?
  • Was there a corrected meter reading or account adjustment?
  • How many billing days are included?
  • Is there an adjustment process for a verified leak?
  • What should I expect on the next statement?

Frequently asked questions

Can my sewer bill increase even if I used less water this month?

Yes. An average, fixed charge or prior-period calculation can make the sewer amount differ from current-month water use.

Does outdoor watering always count toward sewer charges?

No single rule applies. It depends on the utility's billing method and whether separate irrigation metering or exclusions exist.

Can a toilet leak raise both water and sewer bills?

It can when sewer charges are based on metered water consumption.

Can the sewer rate change without my usage changing?

Yes. Rate and fixed-charge changes can raise the total even when usage is similar.

Can BillSavings AI determine the correct local sewer rate?

No. It helps organize statement details so you can compare them with the utility's published billing method.
