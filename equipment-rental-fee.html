Recurring equipment charge

Equipment rental fee on your bill? Check what you are actually renting

Modems, routers, gateways, receivers and other provider-owned devices can create recurring monthly charges. Before changing anything, identify the device, the monthly amount, whether it is required and the provider's return rules.

Review My Bill

Start with the exact device

Modem or gateway

Internet providers may charge for a modem, router or combined gateway. Check whether compatible customer-owned equipment is permitted.

TV receiver or box

Cable and television services may charge per receiver, DVR or additional room device.

Phone or home equipment

Some services include leased hardware, adapters or protection-related devices that appear as a recurring equipment line.

Old equipment still billed

If a device was replaced or returned, compare the return receipt and serial number with the equipment listed on the current statement.

Check whether the equipment is required

Some providers require specific hardware for certain plans, features or support. Others allow customer-owned equipment from an approved compatibility list. A rental fee that looks optional may therefore be tied to a service feature you use.

Ask whether removing the rented device changes technical support, voice service, Wi-Fi features, television recording, security functions or any bundle discount.

Compare rental cost with buying

Multiply the monthly rental amount by twelve to understand the annual cost. Then compare that with the purchase price of compatible equipment, expected lifespan, warranty, support and replacement responsibility.

The cheapest hardware is not automatically the lowest-cost option if it is unsupported, incompatible or needs frequent replacement. Compare the total ownership tradeoff, not only the upfront price.

Internet equipment deserves a separate check

If the rental is for a modem, router or gateway, review our internet equipment fee guide. Some internet plans allow a customer-owned modem but still require provider equipment for specific voice, security or mesh features.

If the internet bill is high overall, use how to lower your internet bill to compare the base plan, equipment, promotional pricing and other recurring charges.

Returned equipment can keep appearing temporarily

A rental charge can remain on a statement if the device return was processed after the billing cutoff or if the returned serial number was not matched correctly to the account. It can also happen when one device was returned but another rented item remains active.

Keep the return receipt, tracking number, date and serial number. If the charge continues, ask the provider to identify the exact device currently associated with the fee.

Replacement equipment can create duplicate confusion

When a modem, router or receiver is replaced, the account may briefly show both old and new equipment records. Compare serial numbers and dates so you know whether the bill reflects one active rental or more than one device.

If the provider charged a service or installation fee during the replacement, review that separately with our service charge guide.

A simple rent-versus-own comparison

Find the equipment name or serial number tied to the fee.

2. Calculate annual rental

3. Check compatibility

Confirm whether customer-owned alternatives are allowed.

4. Compare support

Check warranty, replacement and troubleshooting differences.

5. Review bundle effects

Make sure removing rental equipment does not change another discount or service.

6. Verify the next bill

After a return or equipment change, confirm that the fee disappears as expected.

Questions to ask the provider

  • What exact device does this rental charge cover?
  • What serial number is associated with the fee?
  • Is the device required for my current plan?
  • Can I use compatible customer-owned equipment?
  • Would changing equipment affect technical support or included features?
  • Would it affect a bundle or promotional discount?
  • What is the return process?
  • How do I prove the device was returned?
  • When should the rental line disappear after a return?

Frequently asked questions

Is an equipment rental fee always optional?

No. Some providers or plans require certain equipment, while others allow customer-owned alternatives.

Can returning equipment lower the bill immediately?

Not always. The timing depends on the provider's billing cycle and return processing rules.

Why is the fee still there after I returned the device?

The return may not yet be processed, the bill may have closed before the return, or another rented device may still be active.

Should I keep a return receipt?

Yes. Keep the receipt, date and device serial number until the account confirms the equipment is removed.

Where should I look if several recurring charges appear together?

Use the hidden-fee guide and the BillSavings guide hub.
