Electric bill savings

How to lower your electric bill by understanding what changed

Before cutting usage at random, compare your current statement with older bills. Separate changes in electricity use from changes in rates, delivery charges, billing days and other line items.

Review My Electric Bill

Compare usage and price separately

Your total can rise because you used more electricity, because the price per unit changed, or because fixed and delivery charges increased. Looking at only the final amount can hide which factor matters most.

Check current and prior kilowatt-hour usage, the billing-period length, rate details and any new account charges. This creates a clearer baseline before you make changes.

Where to look for savings

Heating and cooling

HVAC use is often one of the largest household electricity loads. Thermostat settings, filter condition, insulation and equipment efficiency can all affect usage.

Rate plan

Some utilities offer time-of-use, budget or other rate structures. Compare the full terms and your usage pattern before switching.

Recurring account charges

Review service fees, optional programs and other line items so you know which charges are required and which may be elective.

Low-cost steps before major upgrades

Start with obvious waste: unnecessary heating or cooling, lights and devices left on, old filters, air leaks and equipment schedules. If your bill suddenly jumps, first confirm whether the usage measurement changed before buying expensive equipment.

Frequently asked questions

Does BillSavings AI control my thermostat or utility account?

No. It helps organize bill information and highlight items worth reviewing.

Why is my bill higher even though my usage is similar?

Rates, delivery charges, taxes, billing-period length and other fixed costs may have changed.

Should I change my rate plan?

Only after comparing your actual usage pattern, timing, fees and plan rules. A different plan is not automatically cheaper.
