Credit card check guide

What is a convenience check from a credit card company?

A credit card convenience check is a check supplied by a card issuer that lets you draw on your credit account. It can look like a normal check, but the transaction may carry a different APR, transaction fee and interest treatment than an ordinary card purchase.

See what to check first

How a credit card convenience check works

The issuer gives you a paper check connected to your credit-card account. If the check is honored, the amount becomes part of your card balance instead of coming directly from a checking account.

The exact treatment is not universal. Some card agreements treat these checks as cash advances, while other offers may apply promotional or transaction-specific terms. Read the disclosure that came with the checks and your current cardholder agreement before using one.

Four terms to verify before writing the check

APR

Check which annual percentage rate applies. It may be different from your purchase APR.

Transaction fee

Look for a flat or percentage-based fee tied to using the check.

Grace period

Confirm whether interest starts immediately or whether the offer includes any grace-period conditions.

Available credit

Check whether the issuer uses a separate cash-advance or convenience-check limit and whether the check could exceed it.

Convenience check versus convenience fee

These are two different things. A convenience check is a check connected to a credit-card account. A convenience fee is a charge a company may impose for using a particular payment channel.

If you were searching for a fee added when paying by card, use our credit card convenience fee guide.

Why the disclosure matters

Federal credit-card disclosure rules specifically address checks that access a credit-card account. The disclosure can include the type of APR that applies, transaction fees and whether a grace period exists. That makes the paperwork accompanying the checks especially important.

Do not assume that the same terms apply to every convenience-check offer or every issuer. Promotional checks can also carry expiration dates or special conditions.

Before using a convenience check

1. Read the offer

Find the APR, fee, expiration date and any promotional terms.

2. Check the transaction type

See whether your issuer treats the check as a cash advance, balance transfer or another category.

3. Check interest timing

Confirm whether interest begins on the transaction date or whether a grace period applies.

4. Compare alternatives

Compare the total cost with another payment method or financing option before using the check.

Official sources to verify your terms

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau's Regulation Z disclosures explain what issuers must tell consumers about checks that access credit-card accounts, including APR, transaction fees and grace-period information. Individual card agreements can then specify how a particular issuer treats the transaction.

Frequently asked questions

Is a convenience check free to use?

Not necessarily. Check the specific offer for transaction fees and interest terms.

Is it always a cash advance?

No single rule describes every issuer and offer. Some agreements treat convenience checks as cash advances, while special offers can use different terms. Verify your disclosure.

Can the issuer decline the check?

Depending on the account terms and available credit, an issuer may decline a check. Check the issuer's agreement and available-credit limits before relying on it for a payment.

Is this the same as a convenience fee?

No. If a merchant or service provider added a charge for how you paid, see the convenience fee guide.
