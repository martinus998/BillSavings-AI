Review My Recurring Charges

Six common reasons a recurring subscription costs more

Standard price increase

The provider may have raised the regular price for the same plan.

Promotion ended

An introductory, retention or loyalty discount may have expired at renewal.

Plan tier changed

Your account may now include a higher tier, more users or additional features.

Billing frequency changed

Monthly and annual charges can be easy to compare incorrectly if the cycle changed.

Tax or regional pricing

Taxes and location-based pricing can change the final amount even when the base subscription is similar.

Added service

An optional add-on, extra seat, storage upgrade or premium feature may have been added.

Compare the exact old and new recurring price

Use the last normal renewal before the increase and compare it with the current renewal. Separate the base subscription from taxes, add-ons and credits so you can see whether the provider changed the core rate or whether another line caused the difference.

If several subscriptions are changing at once, use our subscription savings guide to organize them before deciding what to keep.

Check the billing cycle before calling it a price increase

A move from monthly to annual billing can create a much larger single transaction even if the monthly equivalent is similar. Conversely, moving from annual to monthly can make the service look cheaper at first while costing more over a full year.

Convert both the old and new plans to the same time period. Annualize monthly plans or divide an annual charge by twelve so the comparison is consistent.

Promotions and credits can disappear quietly

A subscription can keep the same plan name while a promotional credit expires. That makes the total rise even though the standard list price may not have changed during that particular billing cycle.

Look for words such as promotion, credit, loyalty, bundle, introductory or special offer on the prior statement or account history. If the missing credit affected several bills, use how to find hidden fees and missing credits.

Calculate the annual difference

Multiply the monthly increase by twelve or compare the old and new annual renewals directly. Then compare that extra annual cost with how often you use the service and which features you actually need.

A modest monthly increase may be worth reviewing if several subscriptions rise at the same time. Small recurring changes add up when they repeat across a full year.

Before you cancel or downgrade

1. Check renewal terms

Confirm the next billing date and whether cancellation takes effect immediately or at period end.

2. Compare lower tiers

See whether the features you actually use are available on a cheaper plan.

3. Check annual versus monthly

Compare the same time period instead of only the transaction size.

4. Review bundles

Removing one service can affect a bundle discount elsewhere.

5. Check stored data or access

Confirm what happens to saved files, profiles, family accounts or other data if the plan changes.

6. Verify the final bill

After changing or cancelling, confirm the renewal and final charge match what you expected.

Use actual usage to decide what to keep

A subscription that is used daily can justify a different decision from one that has not been opened in months. Check account activity, household usage and duplicated services before focusing only on the percentage increase.

Questions to ask the provider

  • What changed on this renewal?
  • Did the base price increase or did a discount expire?
  • Is the charge monthly or annual?
  • Did my plan tier or number of users change?
  • Are any add-ons included in the new total?
  • Is a lower tier available?
  • Would switching billing frequency change the total?
  • When would a cancellation or downgrade take effect?
  • Are there any refund or proration rules I should review?

Frequently asked questions

Can a provider raise a subscription price at renewal?

Terms vary by service and jurisdiction. Review the provider's notice, agreement and renewal terms for your account.

Can a missing discount cause the increase?

Yes. A promotion or recurring credit can disappear even when the plan name stays the same.

Is annual billing always cheaper?

No. Annual plans may offer a lower effective monthly price, but they require a larger upfront commitment and can have different refund rules.

Should I downgrade immediately?

Compare features, usage, renewal timing and bundle effects before changing plans.

Can BillSavings AI cancel subscriptions?

No. It helps identify recurring charges and organize what is worth reviewing; cancellation must be completed with the provider or billing platform.
