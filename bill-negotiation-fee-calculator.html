Why calculate the fee before signing up?

A lower bill can still be valuable even when a service charges for the result. The useful question is how much you keep after the fee and whether the service saves you enough time or effort to justify that cost.

For example, a recurring monthly reduction can add up across a year, but a percentage-based fee can also be significant. This calculator separates those two numbers so you can compare options on the same basis.

Compare three paths

1. Use a negotiation service

A third party may contact the provider for you. Review its current success-fee or subscription terms before enrolling.

2. Negotiate yourself

You keep any savings, but you handle the research, provider call and follow-up.

3. Review the bill first

Use a bill-review tool to identify recurring charges, price changes and fees before deciding what action to take.

Prepare before contacting the provider

  • Know your current base price and recurring add-ons.
  • Compare the current bill with an older statement or original offer.
  • Calculate the annual impact of recurring price changes.
  • Check whether a promotion or discount ended.
  • Ask what changes would affect equipment, contract terms or bundle pricing.
  • Verify the next bill after any change is made.

Frequently asked questions

Is a percentage fee always bad?

No. The value depends on the amount saved, how long the savings last, how much work the service handles and the exact fee terms.

Should I use this calculator for a one-time refund?

You can model it, but the calculator is designed mainly for recurring monthly savings over a chosen period.

Does BillSavings AI negotiate with providers?

BillSavings AI is a bill-review and planning tool. It helps surface charges and possible savings opportunities so you can decide what to do next.

This calculator is educational and does not provide financial, legal or tax advice. It does not guarantee that a provider will reduce a bill.
