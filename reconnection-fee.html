Reconnection fee on your bill? Rebuild the account timeline

A reconnection or restoration charge may appear after service was interrupted. The useful first step is matching the fee to the interruption, payment and restoration dates and then separating it from any related late, returned-payment or deposit charge.

Reconnection fee versus late fee

A late fee usually relates to payment timing. A reconnection fee relates to restoring service after an interruption. They can appear on the same bill but should be treated as separate charges with separate triggers.

If the account also shows a late charge, compare our late fee checklist or utility late fee guide.

A returned payment can create a chain of charges

A payment may initially look successful and later be reversed. If that leaves the account past due, it can contribute to a returned-payment fee, a late fee and eventually service interruption.

Use our returned payment fee guide to reconstruct the payment timeline before treating each charge as an unrelated event.

Check whether a deposit changed too

Some accounts may show a new or increased deposit after disconnection, restoration or a change in payment history. A deposit is not the same as a reconnection fee, even if both appear after service is restored.

If a deposit line appears, use our utility deposit charge guide and ask whether the deposit is refundable, when it is reviewed and what conditions apply.

Restoration timing matters

Payment submission and service restoration may not happen at the same moment. Some providers restore service automatically after payment posts, while other accounts require manual processing or additional verification.

Ask what event triggered restoration and whether the fee covers a remote account action, field service, after-hours service or another process. The exact provider terminology may differ.

If you believe the fee is wrong

Keep the disconnection notice, payment confirmation, bank record, restoration message and current bill. Ask the provider which policy triggered the charge and what timestamps appear in its account history.

If your payment record shows a different timeline, ask the provider to explain the difference before assuming either record is incorrect.

Questions to ask the provider

  • What event triggered the reconnection fee?
  • What date and time was service interrupted?
  • What payment date and time did you record?
  • Was restoration automatic or manually processed?
  • Is this charge one-time?
  • Did a returned payment or late balance contribute to the interruption?
  • Did the account require a new or larger deposit?
  • Are any additional restoration charges still pending?
  • What recurring monthly total should I expect now?

Frequently asked questions

Can a provider charge both a late fee and reconnection fee?

Policies vary, and the charges can be separate under some provider terms.

Can a returned payment lead to reconnection costs?

It can if the returned payment leaves the account past due and service is interrupted later.

Is a new deposit the same as a reconnection fee?

No. A deposit and a restoration charge are separate line items with different purposes.

Can the fee be removed?

That depends on the provider's rules and account history. Ask what policy applies and whether an adjustment is available.

Does BillSavings AI restore service?

No. It helps organize billing information so you can understand the charge and contact the provider.
