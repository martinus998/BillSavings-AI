Gas bill diagnosis

Why is my gas bill suddenly high?

A higher natural-gas bill can come from more usage, colder weather, a higher rate, a longer billing period or a meter adjustment. Separate those pieces before assuming the cause.

Review My Gas Bill

Six things to compare

Gas usage

Compare therms or other usage units with the previous bill and the same season last year when available.

Weather

Colder periods can sharply increase furnace, boiler or water-heating demand.

Rate

Check the supply or commodity rate separately from delivery and service charges.

Billing days

A longer cycle can produce a higher total even when average daily use is similar.

Meter reading

An estimated reading followed by a corrected reading can make one statement look unusually high.

Appliance changes

A new furnace setting, fireplace, dryer, water heater or extended home occupancy can increase use.

Separate usage from price

If usage jumped, focus on weather, thermostat settings, hot-water demand and appliance operation. If usage stayed similar, compare the rate, delivery charges, fixed fees and billing-period length.

What to do next

1. Verify the reading

Check whether it was actual, estimated or corrected.

2. Compare daily usage

Divide usage by billing days to avoid being misled by a longer cycle.

3. Ask for a line-item explanation

Have the utility explain the rate, delivery charge and any one-time adjustment.

Frequently asked questions

Can cold weather explain a large increase?

It can, especially in heating season, but compare usage and billing days before assuming weather explains everything.

Should I compare dollars or usage first?

Compare both. Usage shows consumption while the total also reflects rates, fees and billing length.

Does BillSavings AI diagnose gas appliances?

No. It helps organize billing information; physical appliance or safety issues require a qualified professional.
