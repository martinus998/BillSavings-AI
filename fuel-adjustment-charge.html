Fuel adjustment charge on your bill: what should you compare?

A fuel or energy adjustment line can change separately from the base rate. The useful check is whether the adjustment rate, usage units or billing period changed — and whether that change actually explains the higher total.

Review My Utility Bill

Break the charge into three parts

Adjustment rate

Look for a per-unit rate, factor or rider and compare it with the prior statement. A higher rate can raise the charge even when usage barely changed.

Usage units

A higher adjustment total can come from higher consumption even when the adjustment rate is unchanged. Compare kWh, therms or the relevant usage unit.

Billing period

More billing days can increase both usage and the total adjustment. Compare the service dates before assuming the rate itself caused the increase.

How to tell whether this line caused the bill increase

Do not compare only the dollar amount of the fuel adjustment. First compare the old and new adjustment rate. Then compare the usage units and billing days. If the rate rose while usage stayed similar, the rate change may explain much of the difference. If the rate stayed similar but usage increased sharply, consumption may be the bigger driver.

If both the rate and usage changed, separate them. Write down the prior and current rate, then the prior and current usage. This makes it easier to see whether the increase came mainly from price, consumption or both.

Do not confuse supply, delivery and adjustments

Electric and gas bills can separate several different cost categories. Supply or commodity charges generally relate to the energy itself. Delivery or distribution charges relate to getting that energy to the property. A fuel or energy adjustment may appear as an additional rate component that changes over time.

If you are reviewing an electric statement, compare our electric delivery charge guide. For natural gas, see the gas delivery charge guide. If the overall total jumped, use our high electric bill checklist, high gas bill checklist or the broader high utility bill guide.

Common reasons the fuel adjustment changes

  • The adjustment rate changed: utilities may update fuel or purchased-energy factors under their applicable rate structure.
  • Your usage changed: the same rate applied to more units creates a higher total charge.
  • The billing cycle was longer: extra service days can increase usage and therefore the adjustment amount.
  • An estimated or corrected reading affected usage: a catch-up reading can shift usage into one statement.
  • The provider changed the label or rate design: compare the tariff or provider explanation rather than assuming a renamed line is a new fee.

If the meter reading looks unusual, use our estimated meter reading checklist. If the statement covers more days than normal, compare the longer billing cycle guide.

A quick four-step check

1. Find the rate or factor

Write down the current fuel-adjustment rate and the rate from the prior statement.

2. Compare usage

Check whether the number of energy units increased, decreased or stayed about the same.

3. Compare billing days

Make sure the current bill does not simply cover a longer service period.

4. Separate other charges

Review supply, delivery, fixed fees, taxes and account charges separately so one line is not blamed for the entire increase.

Questions to ask the utility

  • What rate or factor was used for this charge?
  • Which usage units does it apply to?
  • Did the adjustment rate change from the prior bill?
  • What date did the current factor take effect?
  • Is the line required under the current rate plan?
  • Where can I see the provider's explanation of the adjustment?

Frequently asked questions

Does a fuel adjustment mean the provider made a billing mistake?

No. It can be a normal rate component. The key is verifying how the line was calculated and whether it matches the provider's current rate structure.

Can I lower the charge by using less energy?

If the line is usage-based, lower consumption may reduce the dollar amount, but the adjustment rate itself is generally set by the provider or applicable rate rules.

Why did the charge rise when my usage stayed about the same?

The adjustment rate may have changed. Compare the per-unit factor or rider with the prior statement before assuming your consumption caused the increase.

Can BillSavings AI remove the charge?

No. It can help you identify the line, compare it with prior statements and organize questions to ask the provider.
