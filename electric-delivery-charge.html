What is the delivery charge on your electric bill?

Your electric total may combine energy supply with delivery, distribution, customer and other charges. Separating those pieces helps you see whether a higher bill came from using more electricity or from a change in rates and fixed fees.

Review My Electric Bill

Delivery and supply are not always the same thing

Depending on the utility and market, the supply portion can represent the electricity itself while delivery or distribution covers the local system that carries power to the property. Bills may also include transmission, customer, meter or other regulated charges.

The labels vary, so use the definitions printed on your statement or utility website rather than assuming every provider uses the same structure.

What to compare when the delivery charge changes

Usage

Some delivery charges are partly usage-based, so higher kilowatt-hour consumption can increase both supply and delivery portions.

Rate per unit

Compare the current delivery or distribution rate with the previous statement to see whether the price per unit changed.

Fixed customer charges

A monthly fixed charge can increase the bill even when consumption does not change.

Billing days

A longer billing cycle can raise charges that depend on usage or daily calculations.

Seasonal or approved rate changes

Utility rate structures can change over time. Check current tariff or rate information if the bill does not explain the difference clearly.

How to tell whether usage or pricing caused the increase

Compare kilowatt-hours first. If usage increased sharply, investigate heating, cooling, appliances and seasonal changes. If usage stayed close to the previous period but the delivery portion rose, compare the rate, fixed charges and billing days line by line.

For the broader diagnosis, use why your electric bill suddenly increased. If several utility fees are unclear, see utility bill fees to review.

What to ask the utility

1. What does this line cover?

Ask the utility to define the exact delivery, distribution or customer charge shown on your statement.

2. Did the rate change?

Ask whether the rate, fixed charge or tariff changed since the previous billing period.

3. Is the meter reading actual?

Confirm whether the bill is based on an actual, estimated or corrected meter reading.

Frequently asked questions

Can I remove the electric delivery charge?

Often delivery charges are part of the utility's required rate structure, but rules differ by provider and location.

Can delivery cost more than supply?

It can in some billing structures. Compare the individual line items rather than judging only by the total.

Does BillSavings AI calculate utility rates?

No. It helps organize bill line items so you can see what changed and what to verify with the provider.

Electric rate structures vary by utility, state, market and customer class. BillSavings AI provides informational support and does not determine regulated utility charges.
