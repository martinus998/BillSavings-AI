What is the delivery charge on your electric bill?

Your electric total may combine energy supply with delivery, distribution, customer and other charges. Separating those pieces helps you see whether a higher bill came from using more electricity or from a change in rates and fixed fees.

Review My Electric Bill

Delivery and supply are not always the same thing

Depending on the utility and market, the supply portion can represent the electricity itself while delivery or distribution covers the local system that carries power to the property. Bills may also include transmission, customer, meter or other regulated charges.

The labels vary, so use the definitions printed on your statement or utility website rather than assuming every provider uses the same structure.

What to compare when the delivery charge changes

Usage

Some delivery charges are partly usage-based, so higher kilowatt-hour consumption can increase both supply and delivery portions.

Rate per unit

Compare the current delivery or distribution rate with the previous statement to see whether the price per unit changed.

Fixed customer charges

A monthly fixed charge can increase the bill even when consumption does not change.

Billing days

A longer billing cycle can raise charges that depend on usage or daily calculations.

Seasonal or approved rate changes

Utility rate structures can change over time. Check current tariff or rate information if the bill does not explain the difference clearly.

Meter correction

An estimated or corrected meter reading can shift usage between billing periods and affect usage-based delivery lines.

Fixed versus usage-based delivery charges

Some statements have a fixed monthly customer or distribution charge plus a separate usage-based delivery rate. That means part of the delivery total can remain even when electricity consumption falls.

Compare each line independently. If usage decreased but the fixed component increased, the delivery total may not fall as much as expected.

How to tell whether usage or pricing caused the increase

Compare kilowatt-hours first. If usage increased sharply, investigate heating, cooling, appliances and seasonal changes. If usage stayed close to the previous period but the delivery portion rose, compare the rate, fixed charges and billing days line by line.

For the broader diagnosis, use why your electric bill suddenly increased. If several utility fees are unclear, see utility bill fees to review.

Why a longer billing period can affect delivery

If the delivery portion is tied partly to usage, a 34-day statement can be higher than a 28-day statement even when average daily consumption is similar. Compare the number of days before concluding that the delivery rate itself increased.

Use our longer billing cycle guide when cycle length changed substantially.

Estimated readings can create confusing delivery totals

If a prior bill was estimated and a later bill uses an actual reading, usage can be shifted from one statement to another. The delivery line may rise simply because more kilowatt-hours were assigned to the current bill.

For this pattern, compare our estimated meter reading guide.

Supply switching does not always remove delivery

In some markets, customers can choose an electricity supplier while the local utility still handles delivery. Changing the supply side may therefore leave the delivery or distribution portion in place.

Before changing plans or suppliers, compare the full bill structure rather than assuming the delivery charge will disappear.

A six-step delivery-charge review

1. Find the exact label

Identify delivery, distribution, transmission or customer charges separately.

2. Compare usage

Check kilowatt-hours against the prior period.

3. Compare the rate

See whether the usage-based delivery price changed.

4. Compare fixed charges

Check customer and meter-related amounts separately.

5. Check billing days and meter type

Longer cycles and corrected readings can change the total.

6. Verify the next bill

If the increase was temporary, confirm that the delivery total returns to the expected pattern.

Questions to ask the utility

  • What exactly does this delivery or distribution line cover?
  • Which part is fixed and which part depends on usage?
  • Did the delivery rate change since my prior bill?
  • Did the fixed customer charge change?
  • Was the meter reading actual, estimated or corrected?
  • How many billing days are included?
  • Would changing the supply plan affect this delivery charge?

Frequently asked questions

Can I remove the electric delivery charge?

Often delivery charges are part of the utility's required rate structure, but rules differ by provider and location.

Can delivery cost more than supply?

It can in some billing structures. Compare the individual line items rather than judging only by the total.

Can a fixed delivery charge stay the same if I use less electricity?

Yes. A fixed customer or distribution component may remain even when usage falls.

Can a corrected meter reading raise the delivery line?

Yes, if more usage is assigned to the current statement.

Does BillSavings AI calculate utility rates?

No. It helps organize bill line items so you can see what changed and what to verify with the provider.

Electric rate structures vary by utility, state, market and customer class. BillSavings AI provides informational support and does not determine regulated utility charges.
